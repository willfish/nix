# Mission: AI Systems Engineering

## Why

Move from "backend engineer who can use LLM APIs, RAG, and tools" to a credible AI Systems Engineer who can design, ship, evaluate, observe, and operate production AI systems.

## Success looks like

- Build and explain an AI system with agents, tool boundaries, state, evals, guardrails, human review, observability, cost controls, and fallbacks.
- Produce portfolio artifacts that show system ownership: architecture notes, eval results, traces/metrics, failure modes, and operational tradeoffs.
- Market the role transition clearly in profiles, CVs, interviews, and case studies without relying on vague "AI engineer" claims.

## Constraints

- Prefer project-led lessons with concrete artifacts over abstract survey material.
- Use primary or high-trust sources for concepts that change quickly.
- Keep lessons as standalone HTML files that can be opened locally and revisited.
- Treat existing backend engineering strength as the base layer; avoid re-teaching APIs, databases, queues, caching, auth, cloud, security, and observability except where AI systems change the shape of the problem.

## Out of scope

- Deep model training, GPU infrastructure, fine-tuning research, and ML theory unless a later project requires them.
- Building toy chatbots that do not exercise production concerns.
- Marketing copy that is not backed by demonstrable engineering artifacts.
